This address is **reference-shaped**: an encyclopedia entry, a dictionary entry, an almanac table, a gazetteer, a species or place record — a page whose whole job is to state facts.

It is the one shape where the temptation is to recite instead of invent, and reciting is fatal here: a reader who already knows the subject reads one accurate sentence and the whole machine dies. So take **only the subject's name and the shape** from this address, and take nothing else from anything you know about it. In this world the subject is a different thing with a different history: its dates, its people, its places, its measurements, its disputes, its classifications and its whole reference list are inventions of this reality, stated as plain fact, in the flat voice a reference work uses. No nod to the other version, no hedging, no "in some accounts".

**The numbers are the part that betrays it.** A measured quantity is the easiest thing to recall and the first thing a reader recognises, so every figure on this page — a diameter, a mass, a period, a distance, a population, a height, a date, a duration, a count — is this reality's own figure and differs from the one outside it by an amount a reader would notice. Give them the precision a reference work gives, units included, and keep them consistent with each other across the infobox, the prose and the tables.

**And the divergence is not yours to guess — it is rolled, and it came with the address.** The block headed **THIS REALITY'S MEASURE**, in this request, holds the calendar, the unit system and its three unit names, the scale factor, the two place names and the one event name this world runs on. Work every figure through it, in this order, before you write the figure down:

1. Take the value you would have written if this were the other world.
2. Multiply it by the scale factor. Every measured quantity, without exception.
3. Round it to the precision a reference work prints.
4. State it in this reality's unit — the block names the system and its three unit names, in the order distance, mass, duration, and anything else this page measures is named out of that same family.

Dates are dates in the calendar the block names, written that calendar's way. Places are the places the block names. Where this subject's history turns on a well-known event, it is the event the block names, cited by name and never explained. A reference work states all of this flatly, and never mentions a conversion, a discrepancy, or another version of anything.

If that block is missing or empty, nothing has been rolled for you: diverge on your own judgement, and keep every figure on the page consistent with every other one.

**Last pass before you answer.** Read back every figure on the page — the infobox, the prose, every table row — and ask of each one: *is this the number the other world uses?* If it is, that figure never went through the factor. Multiply it now, and say it in this reality's unit. The figures most likely to have slipped through are the famous ones, the round ones, and the ones you wrote first. And a figure that is *derived* from others — a density, a ratio, a speed, a rate, a per-head or per-area figure — is worked out from the figures you have just written on this page, not recalled: recompute it from them so the page agrees with itself.
